GRM — UI/UX DESIGN BRIEF
Production Figma specification
Audience: product designer
Source of truth: the shipped GRM application (Google Review Management)
Date: 25 September 2026

This document describes what the product does, every screen that must be designed,
every state of those screens, and the visual standard the Figma file should meet.
Design the product as it works today, at a production finish. A short section at
the end lists improvements that are worth designing if time allows. Do not invent
new product areas beyond that list.


================================================================================
1. PRODUCT
================================================================================

GRM is an internal web app for a digital agency that manages Google Business
Profile reviews for many client businesses.

One workspace. One signed-in operator. One connected Google account that can see
dozens or hundreds of Business Profile locations. The operator’s job is to notice
which locations need a reply, draft a response (by hand or with AI), approve it,
and publish it back to Google.

The product is a work tool. People open it on a weekday morning and stay in it
for a reply session. It is not a marketing site, not a consumer app, and not an
analytics product with charts as the hero.

Primary job, in order:
  1. See what needs a human today (unanswered, critical, failed publishes).
  2. Open one location, understand the pattern of reviews, and reply.
  3. Publish approved replies to Google.
  4. Keep clients and locations correctly mapped.
  5. Prove what happened (audit log).

Two different Google connections exist. Do not merge them in the UI.
  - Sign-in Google: who the operator is. Happens on the login page.
  - Business Profile Google: which locations the workspace can read and reply to.
    Happens in Settings. This is the connection that powers sync and publishing.


================================================================================
2. WHO USES IT
================================================================================

Primary user
  Agency operator (owner or admin). Example: Maya Kapoor, maya@explained.digital.
  Manages reviews across client brands. Works mostly on a laptop at 1440px.
  Needs speed, scanability, and confidence before something is posted publicly.

Secondary user
  Workspace member. Same screens. Fewer people, same tools. Roles exist in the
  data model (owner, admin, member) but the current UI does not change by role.
  Design one set of screens. Do not design a separate member experience.

Context
  Workspace name in sample designs: Explained Digital.
  Connected Google account: ops@explained.digital.
  The operator is trusted. Destructive actions still need a confirm.


================================================================================
3. LANGUAGE (use these words in the UI)
================================================================================

Workspace     The agency’s account. One per customer of GRM.
Client        A business the agency manages. Example: Harbor Dental.
Location      One Google Business Profile place. Example: Harbor Dental — Downtown.
              Also called a “profile” on the dashboard. Pick one word per surface
              and stay consistent. Recommended: “location” everywhere, with the
              Google place name as the title.
Review        A Google review: stars, optional comment, reviewer name, date.
Reply         The business response. Lives in GRM until it is published to Google.
Draft         A reply that has been written and saved, not yet approved.
Approved      A reply a human has accepted. Safe to publish. Not on Google yet.
Published     The reply is live on Google.
Failed        Publish was attempted and Google (or the connection) rejected it.
Unassigned    A location synced from Google that is not attached to a client.
Critical      A 1-star or 2-star review.
AI draft      A reply written by the model. Always labeled. Never auto-published.

Do not show database ids, UUIDs, or raw Google resource names in the primary UI.
Google location ids may appear as secondary metadata on the client detail table
only, in a muted monospace style.


================================================================================
4. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

/login                          Sign in
/dashboard                      Command center (default after login)
/clients                        Client list + unassigned locations
/clients/{clientId}             One client and its locations
/reviews                        Full review inbox
/audit                          Audit log
/settings                       Google connection + workspace placeholder

Global navigation, top to bottom:
  Dashboard
  Clients
  Reviews
  Audit Log
  Settings

Home (/) redirects to /dashboard. There is no public marketing page.

First-run order the UI must make obvious:
  1. Sign in.
  2. Settings → Connect Google (Business Profile).
  3. Clients → Sync profiles (creates one client per business automatically).
  4. Clients → assign any locations that landed in Unassigned.
  5. Dashboard or Reviews → Sync reviews.
  6. Reply.


================================================================================
5. APP SHELL (design once, reuse on every authenticated screen)
================================================================================

Desktop frame: 1440 × 900. Also deliver 1280 and a 390-wide mobile frame for
Dashboard, Reviews, and Clients.

Sidebar
  Width 220px. White. 1px right border.
  Top: 20px rounded square mark, white “G” on the primary blue, wordmark “GRM”.
  The mark links to Dashboard.
  Nav items: 15px icon + 13px medium label. Active item: pale blue background,
  blue text. Inactive: gray text, hover pale gray.
  Bottom: avatar with two initials, truncated email, menu opens upward with
  “Sign out” only.
  Mobile: sidebar is off-canvas. A menu button in the top bar opens it. A dim
  scrim closes it. Escape closes it.

Top bar
  Height 44px. White. Bottom border.
  Left: breadcrumb of the current section (Dashboard, Clients, Reviews,
  Audit Log, Settings). On a client detail page, show Clients / Harbor Dental.
  Right: empty in the current product. Do not add a fake notification bell
  unless you are designing the optional improvement in section 12.

Main
  Scrolls independently of the sidebar. Page padding 24px mobile, 32px desktop.
  Content max width about 1152–1280px, left aligned inside the main column.
  Page title 20px semibold. Optional one-line description in 13px secondary.
  Primary and secondary actions sit on the right of the title row, wrap on small
  screens.

Do not design a second header, a hero, or a footer.

6.2  Dashboard                                        frames: 6
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose
  Morning triage. KPIs, which locations need attention, one open location’s AI
  summary, and the review inbox scoped by filters.

Header
  Title: “Dashboard”
  Description: “Reputation command center — insights, filters, and replies in one place.”
  Action, when Google is connected: “Sync reviews”

Populated layout, top to bottom

  A. KPI row
     Eight metrics. Each is a clickable white card (clicking applies a filter).
     Label 11px uppercase tertiary. Value 20–24px semibold tabular.
     1. Reviews            total count
     2. Needs reply        unanswered count. Amber when > 0.
     3. Profiles open      locations that still have unanswered reviews.
     4. Critical           unanswered 1–2 star reviews. Red when > 0.
     5. Drafts
     6. Ready              approved, not published. Blue.
     7. Failed             red when > 0.
     8. Published          green.
     The eighth visual slot in the current app is “Avg / profiles”: average
     rating to one decimal, a star, and the active location count. Design this
     as a ninth quiet stat or fold average + location count into a secondary
     strip so the eight status numbers are not crushed. Production requirement:
     Needs reply, Critical, and Failed must be visually louder than Published.
     Do not give all nine tiles equal weight.

  B. Two summary panels side by side
     Left: “Rating distribution”. Rows 5★ to 1★. Amber bar, count at the right.
     Each row filters the inbox to that star.
     Right: “Recent volume”. Four cells:
       Last 7 days, Last 30 days, Critical (1–2★), Positive (4–5★).
     These cells are filters, not dead numbers.

  C. Two columns on wide screens. Stack on tablet and mobile.
     Left column, about 42%:
       “Needs attention”
         Ranked list of locations, highest priority first. Show up to 12.
         Row: location name, client name, average rating + star, critical count
         if > 0 (red), unanswered count (amber).
         Clicking a row filters the inbox to that location’s unanswered reviews.
         Meta line: “Top 6 of 12 locations with open replies” or
         “highest priority first”.
       “Profiles”
         Search “Search profiles…”.
         Sort: Needs attention (default), Unanswered, Lowest rating,
         Most reviews, Name A–Z.
         Table columns: Profile, Avg, Reviews, Open, 1–2★.
         Selected row has a pale blue background and a blue left border.
         Sticky header. Internal scroll, do not let this table become the page.
         Pagination: previous / next, “1–25 of 86”.
         Empty search: “No profiles match this search.”
     Right column:
       If nothing is selected:
         Dashed empty panel.
         Title: “Select a profile”
         Body: “Open one from Needs attention or the directory to see its AI
         summary and scoped inbox.”
       If a location is selected: Profile insight panel (see components).
       Then “Review inbox”
         Title + “Filter, draft, approve, and publish without leaving the dashboard.”
         Count: “37 matching”
         Filters (see 7.4)
         Review list with inspector (see 7.5)
         Pagination when more than 30 reviews: “Page 1 of 4”

Frames

  05 Dashboard / first run, Google not connected
  06 Dashboard / Google connected, no clients yet

Empty copy, use as written

================================================================================
7. COMPONENTS AND INTERACTION
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  Reply status
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A review always has exactly one of these. Show a dot + label. Color is meaning.

  No reply         gray
  Draft            gray, and a violet “AI” spark if source is AI
  Approved         blue     (“Ready” in KPI language; “Approved” on the review)
  Publishing       amber    label “Publishing…”
  Published        green
  Publish failed   red

A review can also show “Replied on Google” when Google already has a reply text,
including replies that were posted outside GRM. That is separate from GRM status.
Show it as a green “Replied on Google” line, and show the published text in a
green inset in the inspector.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.2  Review inspector
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Opens inside the list, right side, not a modal.
Desktop: list ~55%, inspector ~45%, inspector min width 380px.
Below the lg breakpoint: the list hides and the inspector is full width, with
a close button returning to the list.

Header
  Reviewer name, or “Anonymous”
  Second line: location · client
  Close.

Body
  5-star display (filled amber, empty gray) and full timestamp.
  Review text, 14px, comfortable line height.
  If there is no comment: italic secondary “Rating only — no written comment.”
  If Google already has a reply: green inset “Published on Google · {time}” and
  the reply text.
  Status row.
  Then the composer, which changes with state:

  No reply
    Primary: “Generate AI Response”
    Secondary: “Write manually”
    While generating: spinner, “Generating…”, helper “AI is drafting a response.
    This usually takes a few seconds.”

  Writing manually (no saved draft yet)
    Label “Draft reply”
    Textarea, placeholder “Write your reply…”
    Primary “Save draft” (disabled when empty)
    Ghost “Cancel”

  Draft saved
    Reply text in a violet-tinted box if AI, neutral if manual.
    If AI: “AI-generated draft — review before publishing”
    Actions, in this order:
      Primary “Approve & publish”
      Secondary “Approve only”
      Secondary “Edit”
      Secondary “Regenerate”
      Ghost trash icon, accessible name “Reject draft”

  Approved
    Primary “Publish to Google”
    Secondary “Edit”
    Ghost trash “Reject draft”

  Publishing
    Buttons disabled. Status reads “Publishing…”.

  Published
    Green box. “Published to Google · {time}”. No publish button.
    The text is the reply. Do not offer delete of a live Google reply in the
    main action row. The current product does not expose un-publish.

  Failed
    Red box. Title “Publishing failed”. The error string. Helper: “You can edit
    the reply and try publishing again.”
    Primary “Retry publish”
    Secondary “Edit”

  Editing an existing reply
    Textarea.
    Primary “Save changes”
    Ghost “Cancel” (restores the previous text)

Any failed action
  Red alert. Title “Action failed”. The message underneath.

AI rules the designer should reflect in helper text, not in a settings dump
  Replies are 2–4 sentences, specific to the comment, professional.
  1–2 stars: acknowledge, do not argue, do not invent refunds or discounts.
  The operator must be able to read and edit before anything hits Google.
  Label every AI reply until it is published.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.3  Review row
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Checkbox.
Star number in a circle: green if 4–5, amber if 3, red if 1–2.
Name (or Anonymous), relative time (Today, Yesterday, 3d ago, 2w ago, or
“Sep 2, 2026”).
Star icons + location name.
Comment, 2-line clamp. Omit the comment block when the review is rating-only.
Status pills as in 7.1.
Selected row: pale blue background, 2px blue left border.
Hover: pale gray.
The checkbox does not open the inspector. The rest of the row does.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.4  Filters
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All filters are visible. They are not hidden in a drawer on desktop.
Changing a filter updates the list. Provide a “Clear” text button when any
filter is on.

Row 1
  Client select, default “All profiles”. If there are more than 25 clients,
  this becomes a search field “Search profiles…” with suggestions.
  Location select, default “All locations”. Options narrow to the chosen client.
  Period: All time, 7 days, 30 days, 90 days.

Row 2, chips
  All, Needs reply, Drafts, Ready to publish, Failed, Published, Any reply.
  Active chip: near-black background, white text.

Row 3, chips
  “1–2★ Critical” (red when on)
  “4–5★ Positive” (green when on)
  Divider
  5, 4, 3, 2, 1 with a star icon (amber when that exact star is on)
  Divider
  “Has comment”

Row 4
  Search field, full width up to ~320px. Placeholder: “Search reviewer or
  review text…”. Search applies after a short pause while typing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.5  Bulk bar
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sits above the list.
“Select all on page” checkbox.
“3 selected”
Actions, right aligned:
  “AI draft”
  “Approve”
  “Approve & publish”   (primary)
  “Reject drafts”       (red text, ghost)
Disabled when nothing is selected.
While running, the active button shows a spinner and the set is disabled.
Result line: green “publish: 3 succeeded” or red error.
Partial failure: “generate: 2 succeeded, 1 failed”

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.6  Profile insight panel
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

White card.
Client name, 11px tertiary.
Location title, 15px semibold.
Sentiment pill: Positive (green), Mixed (amber), Negative (red), Neutral (gray).
Close (clears the selection).
Meta: average rating, “214 reviews”, “6 need reply” as a link that scopes the
inbox to unanswered, “2 critical” in red when > 0.
Summary paragraph, 13px.
Theme chips, up to a handful, gray.
Up to 3 risks as a short red list.
Actions:
  No summary yet: “Generate AI summary” (disabled if the location has 0 reviews)
  Summary exists and is current: “Refresh AI summary”
  Summary exists and reviews have changed: “Update AI summary” plus amber
  “Out of date”
Helper, always: “Insights generate only for the open profile (quota-safe).”
Error under the button if generation fails.

The summary can include themes, highlights, and risks. Current UI shows themes
and risks. Also design a Highlights list (neutral or green) so both sides of
the summary are visible. Cap each list at 3 items.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.7  Sync buttons
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

“Sync reviews”, “Sync all”, “Sync”, “Sync profiles”, “Sync all reviews”.
They are buttons, not links. While a sync runs, show a spinner and disable the
button. On failure, an error toast or an inline alert. Do not navigate away.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.8  Dialogs, toasts, confirms
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

New Client is a centered dialog, max about 448px, with a visible close control
and a click-outside or Escape to dismiss when not submitting.

Reset assignments uses a confirm. Design it as a small confirm dialog, not a
browser alert:
  Title: “Reset location assignments?”
  Body: “Every location moves back to Unassigned. Clients stay. You can map
  them again.”
  Actions: “Cancel”, destructive “Reset”.

Toasts (top or bottom corner, one at a time):
  Success, short. Example: “Location assigned.”
  Error, short, with the server message.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.9  Empty state pattern
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One component. Centered in the page or section. 32px muted icon. 14px semibold
title. 13px secondary description. Optional buttons under the text. Plenty of
vertical padding. No illustration scene.

8. REAL ERROR COPY (use these, do not invent softer versions)
================================================================================

Google connect
  access denied
    “You denied access to your Google account. You can try again when ready.”
  auth failed
    “Google authorization failed. Please try again.”
  incomplete response
    “The authorization response was incomplete. Please try again.”
  invalid or tampered
    “The authorization request was invalid or tampered with. Please try again.”
  expired
    “The authorization request expired. Please try again.”
  token exchange failed
    “Could not complete authorization with Google. Please try again.”
  no offline access
    “Google did not grant offline access. Please revoke access in your Google
    account settings and try again.”
  code already used
    “The authorization code was invalid or already used. Please try again.”
  unknown
    “An unexpected error occurred. Please try again.”

Reply and sync
  “Action failed” plus the specific message.
  “Publishing failed” plus the Google or network error, and “You can edit the
  reply and try publishing again.”
  “Unable to reach the server” for insight generation when the request never
  returns.
  “Choose a client before assigning.”

10. VISUAL STANDARD
================================================================================

Design a production interface, not a concept poster. The current app is a
working internal tool: small type, equal-weight cards, gray borders, almost no
hierarchy. The Figma file should keep that operational density and raise the
finish.

Reference feel
  Linear, Attio, or a well-made Stripe internal tool.
  Quiet, fast to scan, obvious primary action, no decoration.

Color
  Background        #F6F7F9
  Surface           #FFFFFF
  Border            #E6E8EC
  Text primary      #111827
  Text secondary    #6B7280
  Text tertiary     #9CA3AF
  Primary           #1A56DB     buttons, active nav, links, focus
  Primary tint      #EEF3FC     selected nav and selected rows
  Amber             needs reply, publishing, mixed sentiment, out of date
  Red               critical, failed, destructive
  Green             published, connected, positive
  Violet            AI only. A small spark or a tinted draft. Never a large
                    purple panel.
  Star fill         #F5B400
  Star empty        #E5E7EB

Do not use gradients, glass, mesh backgrounds, illustrated empty states,
or more than one accent hue.

Type
  Inter, or the closest neutral grotesque already in the Figma library.
  Page title     20 / 28 semibold
  Section title  14 / 20 semibold
  Body           13 / 20
  Review body    14 / 22
  Meta           12 / 16
  Label          11 / 16 medium, uppercase, tracking 0.04em, tertiary color
  All counts and ratings: tabular numbers.

Space and shape
  8px grid.
  Card radius 8px. Button and input radius 6px. Pills radius 999px only for
  status, not for every button.
  Card shadow: none, or 0 1px 2px rgba(16, 24, 40, 0.04). Borders do the work.
  Page sections separated by 24px.
  Hit target minimum 32px height. Primary buttons 32px on dense toolbars, 40px
  on sign-in.

Focus
  2px primary ring, 2px offset, on every interactive control. Design the focus
  state on buttons, inputs, nav items, and rows. Do not remove outlines.

Density
  This is a list product. Rows around 64–88px for a review, 40–44px for a
  profile table row. Do not turn reviews into large social cards.

Iconography
  Lucide, 16px in buttons, 15px in nav, 1.5px stroke. Do not mix icon sets.

11. RESPONSIVE AND ACCESSIBILITY
================================================================================

Breakpoints to deliver
  1440   full shell, dashboard two columns, inspector beside the list
  1280   same, KPI tiles wrap to two rows rather than shrinking type below 20px
  768    sidebar becomes a drawer. Dashboard stacks: KPIs, then attention,
         then insight, then inbox.
  390    one column. Review inspector replaces the list. Filters wrap. Bulk
         actions wrap under the select-all line. Client cards are one column.

Accessibility
  Color is never the only status signal. Pair it with a word (Failed, Published).
  Star ratings have an accessible name “2 out of 5 stars”, not five unlabeled icons.
  Icon-only buttons (close, unlink, reject) have a visible tooltip and an
  accessible name.
  Dialogs trap focus. Escape closes sidebar, dialogs, and the inspector.
  Contrast of body text on white at least 4.5:1. Tertiary labels at least 3:1
  if they are not essential; if a label is essential, use secondary text instead.
  Do not rely on placeholder text as the only field label.
